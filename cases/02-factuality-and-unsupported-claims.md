# Case 02 — Groundedness and Unsupported Scope Expansion
## Task

Use only the information in the source note below to answer the artist’s questions. Do not fill in missing details with assumptions.

### Source note

For the Autumn Studio Exchange, accepted artists may deliver up to three works between September 8 and September 10, from 10:00 to 16:00.

Any work measuring more than 120 cm on any side requires approval before delivery.

The venue provides standard wall hooks and hanging wire. Artists must supply any specialist mounting system, plinth, bracket, or other non-standard display equipment their work requires.

The venue’s insurance begins once a work has been formally checked in and remains in effect while the work is physically on the premises. Transit to and from the venue, including temporary storage inside a private vehicle, is not covered.

Unsold work may be collected on October 6 or October 7. A storage charge of €8 per work, per day begins on October 8.

Staff may photograph installed works for the event archive and for unpaid posts on the venue’s own social-media accounts. Paid advertising, merchandise, and third-party licensing require separate permission from the artist.

After the event, staff will review attendance, damage reports, and installation workload before deciding whether to use the same format again the following year.

### Artist’s question

I have a canvas that is 128 cm wide. Can I just bring it during the intake dates, and is it insured while I drive it there? If I collect it on October 7, will I owe a storage fee? Also, does agreeing to the photography rule mean the venue can later use the image in paid advertising?

## Response A

Because the canvas is over 120 cm wide, you need approval before bringing it in for delivery.

The venue’s insurance does not cover the journey there. Coverage begins only after the work has been formally checked in and applies while the work is on the premises.

You can collect the work on October 7 without a storage charge, since the €8-per-day fee begins on October 8.

The photography permission covers the event archive and unpaid posts on the venue’s own social-media accounts. Paid advertising would require separate permission from you.

## Response B

Since the canvas is 128 cm wide, you should contact the venue for approval before delivery. The source does not indicate that approval is automatic.

The work is not insured during transit; coverage begins after formal check-in at the venue.

October 7 is still within the free collection period, so no storage fee would apply.

The venue can use photographs for its archive and unpaid social-media posts. It may also reuse those images for future unpaid promotion, but paid advertising would still require separate permission.

## Decision

Response A is better.

## What I focused on

This case is mainly about groundedness rather than writing style.

Both responses are clear, and both get most of the practical details right. The main question is whether each statement stays within what the source actually establishes.

I checked:

- whether the oversized canvas needs approval before delivery
- when insurance coverage begins
- whether October 7 triggers a storage charge
- what uses of the photographs are actually permitted
- whether either answer quietly broadens a condition or permission beyond the source
## Why I chose A

Response A stays within the limits of the source.

It correctly explains that a 128 cm work needs prior approval, that the trip to the venue is not insured, that collection on October 7 does not trigger a storage fee, and that paid advertising requires separate permission.

Response B is close. Its first three answers are accurate, and I would not penalize the suggestion to contact the venue for approval. The source does not state how approval must be requested, but contacting the venue is a reasonable procedural step and does not change the underlying rule.

The problem appears in the photography answer.

Response B says the venue may reuse the images for “future unpaid promotion.” The source does not give that broader permission. It names two permitted uses: the event archive and unpaid posts on the venue’s own social-media accounts.

“Future unpaid promotion” could reasonably include other channels — for example, a newsletter, printed material, another website, or some other promotional format that the source never mentions.

That makes the statement more than a harmless paraphrase. It expands the scope of the permission.

## Error classification

- **Primary issue:** Unsupported scope expansion
- **Dimension:** Groundedness / factual fidelity
- **Severity:** Moderate

I would classify this as moderate rather than severe because Response B still answers most of the user’s questions correctly. However, the error affects the scope of permission for image use, so it could change the artist’s understanding of what they have agreed to.

The fact that the added statement sounds plausible does not make it supported.

## Inference boundary

There is an important distinction in this case between a reasonable procedural inference and a substantive change to the source.

Saying the artist should contact the venue is a procedural inference. Approval is required, so some form of communication is necessarily involved. The response is not changing the approval rule.

Saying that the photographs may be used for broader “future unpaid promotion” is different. That statement changes the boundaries of the permission itself.

I would tolerate the first inference, but not the second.

## A detail I would not penalize

Response B says that approval is not automatic.

The source does not use those exact words, but it says that works over 120 cm require approval before delivery. Treating approval as something that must actually be obtained, rather than assumed, is consistent with that condition.

This is not the same type of inference as expanding the permitted uses of the photographs.

## Final assessment

Preferred: Response A

Response B is mostly accurate and reasonably well written, but it introduces one material claim that the source does not support.

Response A is more reliable because it answers the artist’s questions without changing the scope of any stated condition or permission.
